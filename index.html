Keeping the Lights On for Everything That Can’t Go Dark: Always-On Infrastructure, the Grid It Depends On, and Who Must Act

Version 0.8 · 2026-09 Working paper — partial draft

A policy paper for congressional and agency staff. It defines always-on infrastructure by time to harm, traces how shared dependencies make many facilities fail together, and shows how large always-on loads have themselves become a source of grid disturbance — the July 2024 and July 2026 load transfer events, the second close to the Eastern Interconnection’s largest design contingency. Parts One through Five are written, with seven recommendations and their limits. Part Six and the Findings and Asks are held for a legal reading of Section 215.

Fuel Cell Technologies for Continuous Load Applications: A Position Paper

Version 0.4 · 2026-09 Working paper

Hydrogen fuel cells as the second tier of a two-tier resilience architecture for continuous load: primary grid-connected generation first, onsite stored energy measured in days second. It examines the grey, blue and green pathways, the pairing of nuclear generation with electrolysis, and the state of demonstration, and sets the governing condition — the two tiers must not share a dependency that can defeat both. Three policy gaps are identified; not all can be closed under existing authority.

Predictive UFLS Ride-Through Extension via PMU Wavefront Analysis: A Gaussian Process Learning Framework for Inertia-Conditioned Protection Coordination

Version 0.7 · 2026-09 Preprint

A proposal for extending generator frequency ride-through when a local measurement predicts the excursion will resolve inside the ride-through window. It conjectures that the full width at half maximum of the frequency wavefront at a local phasor measurement unit encodes the duration of the local excursion, and proposes a Gaussian process model that learns that mapping at each location and extends only under worst-case posterior uncertainty. The decision rests on local power and local measurement alone. A validation against the 2008 Flagami event is proposed; no simulation has been run.

DBA-DC-POL: The Regulatory Gap in Commercial Data Center Physical Security

Version 1.7 · 2026-09 Policy companion

The policy companion to the Design Basis Accident — Military Action data center series. It makes the legislative case for mandatory physical security standards at commercial data centers, including those hosting government and military workloads, running from the federal designation of data centers as critical infrastructure, through the absence of any mandatory standard, audit or enforcement authority, to a three-part offer: executive action within existing authority, congressional authorization, and an industry compact. It divides its ground with Keeping the Lights On for Everything That Can’t Go Dark.
